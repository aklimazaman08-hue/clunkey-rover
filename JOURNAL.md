# Clunkey Rover: Mini Autonomous Robot — Devlog

> Clunkey Rover is a small four-wheel-drive autonomous robot designed to explore robotics, embedded electronics, mechanical engineering, CAD, and programming. The robot will use an ESP32 as its main controller, four geared motors for movement, two motor drivers to control the motors, and an HC-SR04 ultrasonic distance sensor to detect obstacles in front of it. When the rover detects an obstacle, the planned behavior is to stop, change direction, and continue moving.

> I am designing the rover's chassis in CAD with four motor mounting locations, space for the electronics and battery, and a front mount for the ultrasonic sensor. I want the design to be compact, functional, balanced, and easy to modify as I learn more about robotics and hardware. The project is inspired by my experience on a FIRST Robotics team and my interest in mechanical engineering and robotics. The name Clunkey Rover comes from Clunkey, a robotics mascot that became meaningful to my team. This project gives me a way to continue experimenting with robotics while learning how mechanical parts, electronics, sensors, CAD, and programming work together to create a complete system.

### 2026-10-01 — Clunkey Rover — Warm-Up Session 1

**4h**

#### Clunkey Rover — Warm-Up Session 1

Today I worked on the initial design and planning for my Clunkey Rover, a mini autonomous robot inspired by my experience with FIRST Robotics and my interest in mechanical engineering and robotics.

I created a public GitHub repository and organized the project into sections for CAD, electronics, firmware, and my development journal. I also created an initial concept sketch to plan the rover's overall shape, chassis, motor placement, sensor location, electronics, and battery compartment. The planned rover will use an ESP32 as its main controller, four geared motors for four-wheel drive, two motor drivers, an ultrasonic distance sensor, and a battery system.

I do not have the physical parts yet, so I focused this session on the design and planning stage rather than physical construction. This helped me think about how the mechanical and electrical systems will work together. I also started thinking about how the rover could use the ultrasonic sensor to detect obstacles and change direction.

My next steps were to develop the CAD design further, plan the electronics system, determine the exact parts needed, and prepare for the eventual physical build and testing.

### 2026-10-04 — Clunkey Rover — Design and Planning Session

**6h**

Today I continued working on the Clunkey Rover design and planning. I organized the Bill of Materials with the planned components, quantities, costs, and purposes while keeping the project within the Hack Club Tier 2 budget.

I also developed the electronics plan for the rover. The design uses an ESP32 as the main controller, two TB6612FNG motor drivers, four geared motors, an HC-SR04 ultrasonic distance sensor, and a battery system. The two motor drivers are planned to control the four drive motors, while the ultrasonic sensor will provide distance information to the ESP32.

I continued developing the CAD plan for the four-wheel-drive chassis. The design includes mounting locations for the four motors, space for the ESP32 and motor drivers, a battery compartment, and a front-mounted location for the ultrasonic sensor. I focused on keeping the rover compact and organized while leaving enough room for the components and wiring.

I also continued organizing the project documentation in GitHub, including the CAD, electronics, firmware, journal, and Bill of Materials sections. These documents will serve as a reference when I move from the design stage into the physical build.

The physical parts have not been purchased yet, so the current work is focused on planning and documentation. The next stage will be verifying the exact parts and prices, finalizing the Bill of Materials, and preparing for the physical construction of the rover.
